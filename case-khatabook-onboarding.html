The problem

Merchants signed up, then got stuck on an empty screen.

Most new merchants were first-time smartphone users running small shops. After sign-up, they landed on an empty ledger with no clear next step.

Many never added a customer, so they never saw the app's real value: getting paid faster. Acquisition spend was leaking out of the first week.

Research: what you did and what you learned. Use real quotes from users if you have them.

What we learned

Merchants don't want a ledger. They want their money back.

I ran 20 in-shop interviews across three cities, in Hindi and English, and studied funnel data for the first seven days after sign-up.

Value must be visibleMerchants only cared once they saw a payment come in.Will this get my money back?
Words over iconsMany relied on their own language and familiar words, not symbols.What does this button do?
Trust takes a nudgeSending a reminder felt awkward until the app made it polite.I don't want to annoy my customer.

Key decisions: 2 or 3 choices that shaped the outcome. For each, what you chose, what you rejected, and why.

Key decisions

1. One goal for the first week

Everything in the first week pointed to one outcome: the first payment collected. We dropped a feature tour that tested well in interviews but didn't move activation.

2. Make reminders feel polite

We added friendly, pre-written reminder messages in the merchant's language, so asking for money felt less awkward.

Impact: numbers first, then one quote from a customer or stakeholder.

Impact

More merchants stayed, because they got paid.

Day-7 activation rose by 18%, and twice as many new merchants sent a first payment reminder. The guided first week became the default for all new users.

"The app showed me exactly what to do first."

Kirana store owner, Lucknow

Reflection: what you'd do differently. Shows self-awareness, which hiring managers value in leaders.

Looking back

I'd involve the support team from day one. They heard merchants' confusion every day, and their insights arrived later than they should have.
